A Fantasy Reskin of the Classic Hunter Playbooks

Hunters of the Shattered Coast

No more monsters in the dark. One man rules the Shattered Coast from a black-sailed flagship, and every port, wreck and drowned bell tolls his name. Your company does not hunt beasts any more. You hunt him — his fleet, his spies, his cursed gold, and the weakness that can finally end him.

The hunt has changed: one Pirate Lord, not a monster a week

The shape of the game is the same — hunters follow clues, protect the innocent, and strike when they know their quarry's weakness. What has changed is the quarry: one great enemy, and every voyage peels back another layer of his power.

The shape of a voyage

  1. Word reaches port. A survivor, a wreck, a ransom note nailed to the harbourmaster's door. The company investigates: questioning port folk, reading the signs, following the trail.
  2. The trail narrows. Clues name one of his captains, one of his havens, or one piece of his plan. Expect ambushes, bargains and betrayals on the way.
  3. The strike. Armed with his weakness — a name, a relic, a tide-table, a mutinous first mate — the company strikes. Without the weakness, he slips away laughing, and the Tide turns.
  4. The reckoning. Wounds are bound, tales are told, and the Pirate Lord answers: a port burned, a price raised on your heads, a friend taken.
His weakness is the campaign. The Pirate Lord cannot simply be out-fought — fleets have tried. Somewhere in the Shattered Coast lies the one thing that can end him: the bell that can ring his true name, the admiral he betrayed, the curse that keeps him breathing. Every voyage should reveal, test or protect one piece of it.

Sea beasts and drowned things still sail here — under his flag, or in his wake. Ask whose colours they fly.

Everything modern in the original game has been left on the dock: no guns, no cars, no telephones. Blades, bows, boats and bells only.

Converting your level 13 hero: bring the legend, not the stat block

Convert the fiction, not the numbers. A level 13 hero has a decade of deeds behind them; these sheets capture who they are and what they are famed for, and the veteran start below makes sure they arrive feeling that powerful.

The veteran start — everyone takes all of this:
  • Ratings: pick a line on your playbook, then raise any two ratings by +1 (no rating above +3). Level 13 heroes are good at what they do.
  • Extra moves: take your playbook's starting moves, plus two more moves from its list.
  • Improvements: take three improvements now. One of them may be an advanced improvement (see Ship and company) — including a move borrowed from another playbook, if it fits your story.
  • Luck: start with 6 of 7 luck. You already burned one, long ago, surviving the thing that made you famous.
  • A scar, a famed deed, an old debt: invent one of each — a limp or a hand that shakes near bells; a deed the ports still sing about; a debt someone can collect. Write them on your sheet.

What carries over from your old character

  • One signature treasure. Your best magic item crosses over, translated into tags. A flaming greatsword might be 2-harm hand messy fire; boots of elvenkind become quiet, +1 to sneak. The Chronicler agrees the tags; the item keeps its name and history.
  • Your spellcasting style. Keep how your magic looks and what it costs you. The moves define the effect — you define the flavour.
  • One ally and one enemy. Name a henchman, mentor, lover or contact who still aids you — and one surviving foe. Your old enemy now sails with the Pirate Lord. He made them an offer.
  • Shared history, doubled. When you reach the History section on your playbook, pick two entries for each other hunter, not one. This company has bled together for years.
  • Wealth and standing. You are not rich — the hunt eats gold — but you are known. Ports open gates for your names, and his spies report your movements.

What does not cross over: hit points, armour class, spell slots and saving throws stay in the old world. If your hero could fly, teleport or raise the dead every morning, that power is now rare, costly and story-shaped — agree with the Chronicler what it looks like as a move before the first voyage.

Basic moves

Roll two six-sided dice and add the rating named. 10+ is a clean success. 7–9 succeeds at a cost, a compromise or an ugly choice. 6 or less is a miss: mark experience, and the Chronicler makes a hard move. Ratings: Charm (grace and guile), Cool (nerve under fire), Sharp (wits and eyes), Tough (strength and steel), Weird (magic and the uncanny).

Persuade Someone (+Charm)

Talk a person into something with a promise, a threat, a lie or a song. On 10+ they do it if you keep your word; on 7–9 they want proof or payment first. Against fellow hunters, 10+ gives them experience to agree and +1 forward if they do.

Keep Your Nerve (+Cool)

Act under pressure: swing across on a burning rope, hold a door against boarders, lie to his harbourmaster. On 10+ you do it; on 7–9 the Chronicler offers a worse outcome, a hard bargain or an ugly choice.

Lend Aid (+Cool)

Help another hunter. On 10+ they take +1 to their roll; on 7–9 they still do, but you share whatever goes wrong.

Search for Clues (+Sharp)

Investigate a place, a wreck, a body, a ledger or a rumour. On 10+ ask the Chronicler two questions from the list; on 7–9 ask one, but the search costs time, draws eyes or leaves a trace. What happened here? What is being hidden? What is not what it seems? Where did they go? What can hurt or stop it? What was it guarding?

Read the Room (+Sharp)

Size up a tense moment — a tavern, a parley, a deck before the board. On 10+ ask three questions; on 7–9 ask one. Who is really in command? Who wants to run? What is my best way in or out? Who is most vulnerable? What should I watch for? Acting on an answer gives +1.

Fight (+Tough)

Trade harm in a brawl, a duel or a boarding action. On 10+ deal your weapon's harm and take less in return, or deal +1 harm; on 7–9 you deal harm and take theirs in full. On a miss, you are in their hands.

Guard Another (+Tough)

Shield a person, a place or your ship. On 10+ hold 3; on 7–9 hold 1. Spend hold to suffer the harm instead, keep your charge from harm once, or draw the enemy onto you.

Work Sorcery (+Weird)

Cast, pray, curse or commune. Choose an effect: harm at a distance, heal, bar a place against a named foe, scry a person or place, dispel a curse, summon aid, or bind a spirit. On 10+ it works cleanly; on 7–9 it works, but choose a glitch: it draws attention, it is temporary, it costs you, or it works too well.

Harm, luck and experience

  • Harm. You have 7 harm boxes. Armour reduces harm taken. At 4 or more harm you are unstable: wounds worsen without care. Healing comes from rest, chirurgeons, shrines and sorcery — rarely in the middle of a fight.
  • Luck. Spend a luck point to turn a miss into a 12, as if fate itself stepped in — or to shrug off all the harm from one blow. When your luck runs out, the sea collects: expect the worst, at the worst moment.
  • Experience. Mark experience on every miss, and when a move or your history says so. Fill 5 boxes to level up: erase them and choose an improvement from your playbook.

The Pirate Lord — for the Chronicler's eyes

Give him a name and infamy, a flagship and her tell-tale sign. He is never where the company first looks, he always leaves a witness alive to carry the tale, and he offers each hunter, once, exactly what they most want — at a price. Track his harm like a hunter's, but he only dies when his weakness is used against him. Any other defeat costs him a ship, a captain or a season — and teaches him your names.

What he wants (pick one)

  • The Sunken Crown — a drowned relic that would make him king of every tide
  • Revenge — on the admiral, the court or the lover who betrayed him
  • A throne of fear — every port paying tribute, forever
  • To break his curse — he cannot die, cannot age, and cannot leave the sea
  • Plunder beyond dreams — one last fleet, one last harbour, then legend

His weakness (pick one, hide it well)

  • The harbour bell cast from his first ship — rung at dawn, it speaks his true name
  • The first mate he marooned, alive, who knows every haven and signal
  • His heart, cut out and hidden in a coffer on a charted-nowhere isle
  • The pact itself — a written contract with the drowned god, burnable
  • He cannot set foot on consecrated ground, nor cross a line of salt and ash

The Tide — if the company fails

Advance one step each time a voyage is abandoned, a deadline passes, or a miss demands it. Write your own steps to fit your Lord; these are the shape of them.

  1. Daylight: his sails are sighted off a friendly port
  2. Shadows: a haven is raided; a friend's ship goes missing
  3. Dusk: a port pays tribute or burns; his spy sits in your council
  4. Sunset: his fleet blockades the company's home harbour
  5. Darkness: the relic is raised / the betrayal lands / the curse breaks his way
  6. Midnight: he is crowned Lord of the Shattered Coast — and turns inland

His fleet

Name each captain and threat, give each a kind and a want. Kinds: boarding crew (harm in numbers), hired blades, bribed officials, sea cultists, cursed drowned crew, a rival captain with her own ambitions, the thing in the fog that answers his whistle.
