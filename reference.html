Moves Reference

The short versions of the basic moves live on How to Play. This page is the full condensed reference from the reference sheets: every basic move with its 10+, 7–9, miss and advanced 12+ results, the Use Magic glitch list, and the Alternative Weird moves. Terms are reskinned as on the rest of the site: the storyteller is the Chronicler, monsters are the Pirate Lord and his fleet, a mystery is a voyage, bystanders are port folk.

How to read a move: roll two six-sided dice and add the rating named. 10+ is a clean success. 7–9 succeeds at a cost, a compromise or an ugly choice. 6 or less is a miss: mark experience, and trouble arrives. 12+ is an advanced result — better than a plain 10+, as each move says.

Hunter Agenda

  • Act like you're the hero in this story (because you are).
  • Make your own destiny.
  • Find the damn Pirate Lord and stop him.
  • Play your hunter like they're a real person.

Basic moves — in full

Persuade Someone (+Charm)

Once you have given them a reason, tell them what you want them to do and roll +Charm.

For a normal person: on a 10+, they'll do it for the reason you gave them. If you asked too much, they'll tell you the minimum it would take for them to do it (or if there's no way they'd do it). On a 7–9, they'll do it, but only if you do something for them right now to show that you mean it. If you asked too much, they'll tell you what, if anything, it would take for them to do it.

For another hunter: on a 10+, if they do what you ask they mark experience and get +1 forward. On a 7–9, they mark experience if they do what you ask. On a miss, it's up to that hunter to decide how badly you offend or annoy them. They mark experience if they decide not to do what you asked. The Pirate Lord and his captains cannot normally be persuaded.

Advanced: on a 12+, for a normal person, not only do they do what you want right now, they also become your ally for the rest of the voyage (or, if you do enough for them, permanently). For another hunter, on a 12+ they must act under pressure to resist your request. If they do what you ask, they mark one experience and take +1 ongoing while doing what you asked.

Keep Your Nerve (+Cool)

When you act under pressure, roll +Cool. On a 10+, you do what you set out to. On a 7–9, the Chronicler is going to give you a worse outcome, hard choice, or price to pay.

Advanced: on a 12+, you may choose to either do what you wanted and something extra, or to do what you wanted to absolute perfection.

Fight (+Tough)

When you get into a fight, roll +Tough. On a 7+, you and whatever you're fighting inflict harm on each other. The amount of harm is based on the established dangers in the game: you inflict the harm rating of your weapon, and your enemy inflicts their attack's harm rating on you.

On a 10+, choose one extra effect: you gain the advantage — take +1 forward, or give +1 forward to another hunter; you inflict terrible harm (+1 harm); you suffer less harm (−1 harm); you force them where you want them.

Advanced: on a 12+, instead pick an enhanced effect: you completely hold the advantage — all hunters involved in the fight get +1 forward; you suffer no harm at all; your attack inflicts double the normal harm; your attack drives the enemy away in a rout.

Lend Aid (+Cool)

When you help another hunter, roll +Cool. On a 10+, your help grants them +1 to their roll. On a 7–9, your help grants them +1 to their roll, but you also expose yourself to trouble or danger.

Advanced: on a 12+, your help lets them act as if they just rolled a 12, regardless of what they actually got.

Guard Another (+Tough)

When you prevent harm to another character, roll +Tough. On a 7+, you protect them okay, but you'll suffer some or all of the harm they were going to get.

On a 10+, choose an extra: you suffer little harm (−1 harm); all impending danger is now focused on you; you inflict harm on the enemy; you hold the enemy back.

Advanced: on a 12+, both you and the character you are protecting are unharmed and out of danger. If you were protecting a port-folk bystander, they also become your ally.

Search for Clues (+Sharp)

When you investigate a voyage, roll +Sharp. On a 10+, hold 2; on a 7–9, hold 1. One hold can be spent to ask the Chronicler one of the following questions:

  • What happened here?
  • What sort of creature or foe is it?
  • What can it do?
  • What can hurt it?
  • Where did it go?
  • What was it going to do?
  • What is being concealed here?

Advanced: on a 12+, you may ask the Chronicler any question you want about the voyage, not just the listed ones.

Read the Room (+Sharp)

When you look around and read a bad situation, roll +Sharp. On a 10+, hold 3; on a 7–9, hold 1. One hold can be spent to ask the Chronicler one of the following questions:

  • What's my best way in?
  • What's my best way out?
  • Are there any dangers we haven't noticed?
  • What's the biggest threat?
  • What's most vulnerable to me?
  • What's the best way to protect the victims?

If you act on the answers, you get +1 ongoing while the information is relevant.

Advanced: on a 12+, you may ask the Chronicler any question you want about the situation, not just the listed ones.

Work Sorcery (+Weird)

When you use magic, say what you're trying to achieve and how you do the spell, then roll +Weird. On a 10+, the magic works without issues: choose your effect. On a 7–9, it works imperfectly: choose your effect and a glitch. The Chronicler will decide what effect the glitch has. Advanced: on a 12+, the Chronicler will offer you some added benefit.

Effects (choose one):

  • Inflict harm (1-harm ignore-armour magic obvious).
  • Enchant a weapon. It gets +1 harm and +magic.
  • Do one thing that is beyond human limitations.
  • Bar a place or portal to a specific person or a type of creature.
  • Trap a specific person, cutthroat, or sea-beast.
  • Banish a spirit or curse from the person, object, or place it inhabits.
  • Summon a creature into the world.
  • Communicate with something that you do not share a language with.
  • Observe another place or time.
  • Heal 1-harm from an injury, or cure a disease, or neutralise a poison.

Glitches (choose one on a 7–9): the effect is weakened; the effect is of short duration; you take 1-harm ignore-armour; the magic draws immediate, unwelcome attention; it has a problematic side effect.

The Chronicler may say that… the spell requires weird materials; the spell will take 10 seconds, 30 seconds, or 1 minute to cast; the spell requires ritual chanting and gestures; the spell requires you to draw arcane symbols; you need one or two people to help cast the spell; you need to refer to a tome of magic for the details.

Big Magic

Use this when you want more than the Work Sorcery effects. Tell the Chronicler what you want to do. The Chronicler may require: you need to spend a lot of time (days or weeks) researching the magic ritual; you need to experiment with the spell — there will be lots of failures before you get it right; you need some rare and weird ingredients and supplies; the spell will take a long time (hours or days) to cast; you need a lot of people (2, 3, 7, 13, or more) to help; the spell needs to be cast at a particular place and/or time; you need to use magic as part of the ritual, perhaps to summon a creature, communicate with something, or bar the portal you opened; it will have a specific side-effect or danger. If you meet the requirements, then the magic takes effect.

Instead of Work Sorcery — Alternative Weird moves

Hunters who do not have Work Sorcery as their Weirdness move must treat any spell as Big Magic. The moves below replace Work Sorcery for hunters who take them — they are also pickable on the Hunters sheets for the Battle-Mage, the Witch, the Touched, the Cursed, the Channeler and the Fae-Blooded.

Illuminated

When you ask the Hidden Patrons for aid, roll +Weird. On a 10+, they reveal a key fact, clue, or technique that will help you. On a 7–9, they need you to complete a task for them. Once it is done, they reveal a key fact, clue, or technique that will help you. On a miss, their reply is terrible, garbled, or somehow dangerously wrong. Advanced: on a 12+, they reveal a key fact, clue, or technique that will help you, and you may ask one follow-up question that the Chronicler will answer honestly.

Past Lives

When you channel your previous incarnations to discover something, pose your question and roll +Weird. On a 10+, a past life has something useful to offer — ask the Chronicler two of the questions below. On a 7–9, a past life has a little experience with this — ask one. On a miss, a past life takes over for a while. Advanced: on a 12+, a past life knows exactly what you were after — ask the Chronicler one of the questions below, and one free-form question. Gain +1 ongoing while acting on the answers.

  • What did a past life discover about ___?
  • How did a past life deal with ___?
  • What important hidden secret can a past life show me the way to?
  • What did a past life learn too late to help them?
  • What does a past life advise me to do now?

Sensitive

When you open up your mind to the unseen, roll +Weird. On a 10+, you gain a definite impression (a vision, tangible aura, overheard thought, etc.) about something important. On a 7–9, you gain a hazy impression about something important. On a miss, your mind makes contact with something dangerous. Advanced: on a 12+, you get an impression as for 10 or more, plus you may ask one follow-up question that the Chronicler will answer honestly.

Empath

When you open up your mind to feel the emotions of something right there in front of you, roll +Weird. On a 10+, you gain a clear impression of their current emotional state and intentions. Take +1 forward when acting on this knowledge. On a 7–9, you gain a hazy impression of their current emotional state and intentions. On a miss, your mind is overwhelmed with emotion. Advanced: on a 12+, you get an impression (as for 10 or more), and you may ask one follow-up question that the Chronicler will answer honestly. You can use Empath on anything with emotions, but the less human they are, the less safe it is for you. If the emotions are especially strong or alien, you might need to keep your nerve first.

No Limits

When you push your physical body past its limits, roll +Weird. On a 10+, your body obeys your will, to the limits of physical possibility, for a moment. On a 7–9, you do it but choose one consequence: suffer 1-harm, take −1 forward, or you need to rest right now. On a miss, something goes horribly wrong. Advanced: on a 12+, you can continue acting at your body's limits for 30 seconds. For the purposes of this move, "physical possibility" means "conceivably possible for a human." For example — physically possible: lift a cart, leap a wagon, punch through a door. Not physically possible: lift a ship, fly, punch a blast of energy at a foe.

Telekinesis

When you fling something with your mind, roll +Weird. On a 10+, you move it. Choose two options and mark 1-harm. On a 7–9, you move it but it hurts. Choose one option and mark 2-harm. On a miss, something goes horribly wrong. By default you can move something smaller than a person. You don't have much control, and you can't move it strongly enough to hurt anything.

Options (anything not picked is not true): something is held fast; something is hurt (2-harm smash); something catches fire; you can fling something bigger than a person; you keep it basically under your control; you suffer 1 less harm.

Advanced: when you advance your Telekinesis move, on a 12+, choose three options. You may also choose from these advanced options: something explodes (3-harm close fire area messy); something implodes (3-harm close crush); lots of stuff is flying under your control; you have perfect and precise control over exactly what happens.

Trust Your Gut

When you consult your instincts about what to do next, roll +Weird. On a 10+, the Chronicler will tell you where you should go. Wherever that is, it will be important. You get +1 ongoing on the way to this place. On a 7–9, the Chronicler will tell you a general direction to go. Take +1 forward as you explore that. On a miss, your instincts lead you into danger. Advanced: on a 12+, in addition to the usual 10+ result, the Chronicler will tell you about one important thing you should investigate further.

Strange Craft (Weird Science)

When you create or adapt a device to analyse or deal with strangeness, say what it will do and roll +Weird. On a 10+, you pick two requirements. On a 7–9, you pick one requirement and the Chronicler picks a second one. On a miss, something goes horribly wrong. You are still able to create your device, but the Chronicler picks three requirements. Advanced: on a 12+, you gain +1 ongoing when operating the device.

Requirements: it needs a rare and/or weird material; it won't be very reliable; it requires huge amounts of power or fuel; it will take a long time to get it working; it won't work exactly as intended; you'll need help (beyond the hunters on your team) to finish it.

Phenomena — strange things that are not the Pirate Lord

Some voyages turn on a phenomenon: a cursed relic, a drowned shrine, a patch of wrong sea. Hunters dealing with phenomena may ask the following as part of the standard Search for Clues list of questions: How is this phenomenon doing this? What could fix it, cure it, or slow it down? How far does the effect reach? What will be affected next?

Harm, armour, luck, recovery and levelling

Harm

When you suffer harm, ask the Chronicler what effect it has on you. Injury severity depends on how much harm you have suffered: 0-harm wounds have only minor, short-term effects. 4–7 harm wounds are serious and unstable. They will get worse unless treated — mark the "Unstable" box. 8-harm or more will kill a normal human, including a hunter. (Hunters track 7 harm boxes on their sheet; at 7, a hunter is dying.) Armour reduces the harm suffered by the number of points it is rated for.

Luck

When you spend a point of Luck, pick one: decrease a wound you have suffered to 0 harm; after you roll, retroactively change the result to a 12. When you have no luck left, bad things will happen to you.

Recovery

  • 0-harm wounds are considered healed right away.
  • 1–3 harm wounds improve when you receive first aid, and later when you rest. Heal 1 when you do.
  • Unstable wounds require first aid to become stable. While unstable, they may get worse.
  • 4+ harm wounds require a healing move, time in an infirmary or hospital, or magical healing.
  • At the end of the voyage, you also have a chance to heal: if there is no chance to rest, heal 1 harm; if there is plenty of time, heal all harm.

Levelling up

Mark an experience point whenever your roll totals six or less, or when a move tells you to. Whenever you mark the fifth experience box, level up. Erase all five marks and choose an improvement from your list. After you have levelled up five times, you may choose from the advanced improvement list as well.

End of session

At the end of each session, ask yourselves:

  • Did we conclude the current voyage?
  • Did we save someone from certain death (or worse)?
  • Did we learn something new and important about the world?
  • Did we learn something new and important about one of the hunters?

If you get one or two "Yes" answers, mark one experience. If you get three or four, mark two.
