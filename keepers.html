Hunters of the Shattered Coast

No more monsters in the dark. One man rules the Shattered Coast from a black-sailed flagship, and every port, wreck and drowned bell tolls his name. Your company does not hunt beasts any more. You hunt him — his fleet, his spies, his cursed gold, and the weakness that can finally end him.

Keeper's Korner

This page is for the Chronicler — the storyteller who plays the Pirate Lord, his fleet, and every soul in every port. Hunters, you may read it too, but his weakness is hidden here: knowing it spoils the hunt.

The Pirate Lord — for the Chronicler's eyes

Give him a name and infamy, a flagship and her tell-tale sign. He is never where the company first looks, he always leaves a witness alive to carry the tale, and he offers each hunter, once, exactly what they most want — at a price. Track his harm like a hunter's, but he only dies when his weakness is used against him. Any other defeat costs him a ship, a captain or a season — and teaches him your names.

What he wants (pick one)

  • The Sunken Crown — a drowned relic that would make him king of every tide
  • Revenge — on the admiral, the court or the lover who betrayed him
  • A throne of fear — every port paying tribute, forever
  • To break his curse — he cannot die, cannot age, and cannot leave the sea
  • Plunder beyond dreams — one last fleet, one last harbour, then legend

His weakness (pick one, hide it well)

  • The harbour bell cast from his first ship — rung at dawn, it speaks his true name
  • The first mate he marooned, alive, who knows every haven and signal
  • His heart, cut out and hidden in a coffer on a charted-nowhere isle
  • The pact itself — a written contract with the drowned god, burnable
  • He cannot set foot on consecrated ground, nor cross a line of salt and ash

The Tide — if the company fails

Advance one step each time a voyage is abandoned, a deadline passes, or a miss demands it. Write your own steps to fit your Lord; these are the shape of them.

  1. Daylight: his sails are sighted off a friendly port
  2. Shadows: a haven is raided; a friend's ship goes missing
  3. Dusk: a port pays tribute or burns; his spy sits in your council
  4. Sunset: his fleet blockades the company's home harbour
  5. Darkness: the relic is raised / the betrayal lands / the curse breaks his way
  6. Midnight: he is crowned Lord of the Shattered Coast — and turns inland

His fleet

Name each captain and threat, give each a kind and a want. Kinds: boarding crew (harm in numbers), hired blades, bribed officials, sea cultists, cursed drowned crew, a rival captain with her own ambitions, the thing in the fog that answers his whistle.

Running the hunt

The shape of a voyage

  1. Word reaches port. A survivor, a wreck, a ransom note nailed to the harbourmaster's door. The company investigates: questioning port folk, reading the signs, following the trail.
  2. The trail narrows. Clues name one of his captains, one of his havens, or one piece of his plan. Expect ambushes, bargains and betrayals on the way.
  3. The strike. Armed with his weakness — a name, a relic, a tide-table, a mutinous first mate — the company strikes. Without the weakness, he slips away laughing, and the Tide turns.
  4. The reckoning. Wounds are bound, tales are told, and the Pirate Lord answers: a port burned, a price raised on your heads, a friend taken.

Chronicler's tools

  • Keep him off-stage. He is never where the company first looks. Let his captains, his spies, and his cursed gold do the work, and let him appear only when it costs him something — or them.
  • Advance the Tide on failure, not on time. A voyage abandoned, a deadline missed, a miss that demands it: one step. The Tide is the price of dawdling, not a clock.
  • Offer each hunter, once, exactly what they most want — at a price. That is his favourite weapon, and it works on level 13 heroes better than any cannon.
  • Make every voyage reveal, test, or protect one piece of his weakness. If a voyage does none of those, it is a side-trip: fine, but say so.
  • Use the new playbooks. The Famed Bard's admirers, the Inquisitor's contacts, the Broadsheet's crew, the Vessel's symbiote, the Far-Traveller's strangeness — all of them are hooks. A hunter's special feature is a voyage waiting to happen.

Your ship and company

Ship's name and home port — write them in. Kind — circle one: brig, sloop, cog, longship, cutter.

Her qualities (pick three)

  • Fast — she outruns anything but his flagship
  • Shallow-drafted — creeks and smugglers' coves
  • Heavily gunned for her size
  • Warded against curses and scrying
  • Roomy hold — cargo, prisoners, refugees
  • Unremarkable — no one looks twice
  • A crew of twelve loyal salts (name the mate below)

Hull harm: at 4+, she is breaking up. First mate / crew worth naming: write them in.

Company funds and prize money: track loosely, in marks: a full purse, a lean purse, or debts at the harbourmaster's. Prize money from his captured ships is real income — and every prize crew you sail home is a ship not hunting.

Advanced improvements

Available once you have taken five ordinary improvements — and one is included in the veteran start. Pick from any playbook's list where noted, or:

  • Raise any rating by +1 (max +3)
  • Take a move from another playbook
  • Take a second move from another playbook
  • Gain a haven, a ship upgrade or a sworn ally
  • Change to a new playbook — your legend turns a page
  • Retire this hunter to safety, and bring in their heir, apprentice or old comrade
  • Mark the Pirate Lord himself: you have learned one true thing about his weakness, permanently

Voyage log

Record each voyage, the Tide step it cost, and what it cost. (Use the table in the PDF, p. 18, for a printable log.)

Before the first voyage — checklist

  • Every hero converted: playbook, veteran start, signature treasure, ally and enemy named
  • The Chronicler has built the Pirate Lord and hidden his weakness
  • The company has named its ship and chosen her qualities
  • History done at the table: two entries per hunter, out loud, in character
  • First hook agreed: the wreck, the ransom note, or the bell that rang itself
Fair winds. He has a fleet, a curse and a head start of twenty years. You have each other, one good ship, and everything level 13 taught you. That has always been enough — barely, and at a price. Set sail.
